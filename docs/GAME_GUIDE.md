# Medieval Quest — Game Guide

Medieval Quest is a turn-based game on a map of the known world: each turn is a year, from 1000 to 1700. You lead a historical faction and make it grow through conquest, diplomacy, marriage, religion and trade. Battles are not fought by hand: when two enemy armies meet, the clash is resolved automatically at the end of the year.

In game, press **H** at any time for the list of controls.

*This is the English version of the guide. The Italian original is [GUIDA_DI_GIOCO.md](GUIDA_DI_GIOCO.md).*

---

## 1. Getting started

### Main menu

- **New Campaign**: choose your faction, look at its religion, capital, provinces and starting treasury, its historical strengths and weaknesses, set the difficulty and press "Start Campaign".
- **Continue**: resumes the last game.
- **Load Game**: opens the list of saves.
- **Settings**: AI difficulty, board tilt, music and effects volume.
- **Language**: Italian or English. The choice is kept for later games.

### How to win

- **Domination**: you win when you control 85% of the provinces. You can then end the game or keep playing.
- **End of the campaign**: in 1700 the game ends with a ranking by provinces.
- You lose if your faction dies out or is left with no provinces and no armies.

The **Standings** (G key) show at any time the provinces, income and share of each faction, and how many provinces you still need for domination.

## 2. The screen

- **Board**: the map with provinces, seas, castles, ports and pieces (armies, fleets, agents). You see everything in your own provinces, in the regions where you have pieces and in the neighbouring ones; the rest is hidden by the fog.
- **Top bar**: Court, Diplomacy, Standings, year, treasury, income and wars in progress. Top right: "Rulers" and "Menu".
- **Bottom bar**: minimap (click to move the view; the compass in its bottom-left corner shows all the discovered legendary places), information medallions, cards of the selected province, army or fleet, army commands, treasury and the **End Year** button. Below the building and recruitment cards, "H: commands and keys" opens the list of controls.
- **Mouse hover**: over pieces, seas and provinces a box appears with the owner and relations. Colours: red for enemies, blue for neutrals, green for allies.

## 4. The turn

Give your orders whenever you like, then press **End Year** (or Enter). The turn is always resolved in this order:

1. movement of armies, fleets and agents;
2. agent missions;
3. battles, sieges and assaults;
4. income (farming, mining, trade) and troop upkeep;
5. building and recruitment;
6. events, births, deaths and news from the world.

Important events (battles, conquests, wars, marriages, revolts, disasters) open in illustrated windows and the view moves to the place where they happen. "Skip all" closes them all at once; the report of the year comes afterwards.

A piece with an order appears at once in the destination region, but it only really gets there at the end of the year: until then it cannot be used for anything else.

## 5. Provinces

Each province has a castle, buildings, a population with its own religion, a loyalty level and an income. The province window has the **Province, Castle, Government, Buildings** tabs, plus building and recruitment.

- **Building**: pick a building among those "To build"; with its icon it moves to those "Under construction". The queue holds up to 5 buildings. A click on the tile cancels it. A finished building can be demolished (half the cost is refunded); when a province is conquered, some buildings are destroyed.
- **Recruiting**: the Troops, Fleet and Agents tabs show what you can recruit there, depending on the buildings. With "Repeat the queue" completed units start again on their own; if the treasury runs short the queue pauses and resumes as soon as possible.
- **Weapons and armour**: armourers and smiths improve the troops recruited in the province. The levels appear on the cards: shield (armour) on top, sword (weapons) below.
- **Governor**: in the Government tab you choose who runs the province. His acumen affects income and order.
- **Loyalty**: high taxes, a different religion, foreign armies and enemy spies lower it; garrisons, religious buildings and a good governor raise it. Taxes, garrison and governor take effect at once, without waiting for the end of the year: the tax choice and the list of governor candidates show how much loyalty changes. A discontented province revolts.
- **Trade**: local trade pays even without a port. With ports and fleets guarding the routes you also get sea trade income, which grows with the wealth of the ports reached. An enemy fleet in a sea zone closes it.

### Legendary places

The world map holds 39 historical and mythological places, on land and at sea: ruins, shrines, wonders and perils.

- **Discovery**: a place stays hidden until you see its region. On the map it is a dot; hovering over it opens the medallion with its name, and a click opens its sheet with the painting, the description and the effects.
- **Effects**: whoever holds the province gets bonuses and penalties (income, loyalty, agents, recruits, calamities, castle defence). Some depend on religion or on the year. At sea they apply to whoever crosses the zone or to whoever guards it with a fleet for a few years.
- **Events**: some places offer choices, with a safe option, a risky one and sometimes one with requirements (money, stars, religion). A red seal on the dot means a choice is waiting.

## 6. Armies and fleets

- **Movement**: drag the army onto a region. While you drag, the regions you can reach this year are coloured (green on foot or at sea, light blue by sea transport, red for an attack) and the rest is darkened.
- **Merging and splitting**: drag an army onto another of yours to merge them. To split them, select the cards in the bottom bar and drag them onto the map.
- **Identical units are merged**: when you merge two armies or move units into another army, units of the same type are topped up to full strength with the men of the weaker ones (their valour becomes the average); units left empty disappear. The same happens automatically after every battle. You can also drag a card in the bar onto another card of the same type in the same army: the target card fills up and any extra men stay in the dragged card. Mercenaries are only merged with each other.
- **Attacking**: drag the army onto a foreign one. If the attack starts a new war (from peace, alliance or ceasefire) the game asks for confirmation.
- **Castles**: drag an army onto your own castle to withdraw into it (the units that fit go in: 16 places, cavalry takes two; a fortress holds a whole army of 20 units). On an enemy castle this starts a siege; with the siege under way, the same gesture orders the assault.
- **Commands in the bottom bar**: cancel orders, withdraw, assault, retrain, commander, **Replenish** (brings all under-strength units back to full strength, paying at once) and **Disband** (the units leave the army without a refund and become mercenaries).
- **Commanders**: kings, princes and generals lead armies. A right click on the commander's card opens his sheet with portrait, stars and traits; the "Sheet of the unit they lead" button shows the men, valour, weapons and attributes of his unit.
- **Fleets**: each fleet has an admiral who improves by winning. Every year ships cross as many sea zones as their range. Non-allied fleets, even neutral ones, stop troops from crossing by sea (naval blockade). Storms can sink the weaker ships.
- **Close view**: you see the make-up and strength of foreign armies and fleets in the regions bordering your provinces or where you have ships nearby. For the buildings, loyalty and income of other factions' provinces you need spies.

## 7. Agents

Agents are recruited in the Agents tab and used by dragging them onto a target (or with a right click). If the target is far away, the agent travels for as many years as needed. Agents cross inland seas even without ships, but oceans need a chain of ships.

- **Emissaries**: dragged onto a foreign king, army or emissary, they propose an alliance or a ceasefire; they can also bribe foreign armies (not those led by a king or an heir).
- **Princesses**: dragged onto a foreign heir or king, they propose marriage, which strengthens relations between the factions.
- **Spies**: in your own province they raise loyalty and catch enemy agents; in someone else's they spy on provinces and armies, stir up discontent, sabotage and can open the gates of a besieged castle.
- **Assassins**: they strike generals, nobles and agents; the more stars they have, the more likely they are to succeed.
- **Religious agents** (bishops, imams and others): they convert the population to your religion.
- **Inquisitors**: in your own province they convert heretics; with a trial they can send a Catholic character to the stake.

The **Agents** tab of the spies and messages window lists all your agents; "Go to" moves the view to the selected one.

## 8. Dynasty and court

- The **faction leader** and his heirs grow old, marry, have children and die. If the dynasty dies out, the faction falls.
- In the **Court** (C) the council chamber shows the king in the centre and the offices of state around him: "Grant/Change" and "Revoke" under each seat. Anyone who already holds an office is shown in blue in the selection lists.
- **Prisoners**: after a battle won you can execute the prisoners, release them or ask for a ransom (worth much more for kings, princes and nobles). The other faction decides whether to pay; if it refuses, the prisoners are executed.
- The current **Rulers** (K) are shown with portrait, name and faction.

---

## 9. Diplomacy and religion

- The possible relations are war, peace, alliance and ceasefire (with an expiry date). The **Diplomacy** window (D) shows, for each faction, who it is at war with.
- The computer factions send you proposals (alliances, ceasefires, marriages, ransoms) that you answer with the buttons.
- **Papacy**: for Catholics, the Pope warns those who attack other Catholics and excommunicates those who attack the Papacy or ignore his warnings; a Catholic faction that is not excommunicated can call **crusades**. Muslim factions have the **jihad**. Crusading armies gather new units as they cross lands of their own faith.
- The population's religion affects loyalty and revolts; the religion tint (R) shows the main faith of each region.

## 10. Saving

- **F5** quick save, **F9** quick load.
- From the **Menu** (top right, or Esc): save, load, back to the main menu, quit the game.
- Save files are in `%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\MTW Remake` and are kept even after uninstalling.
- Games saved before version 2026.11.28 do not have the new regions of the world map: to play with them you need a new campaign.

---

## 11. Common problems

- **Black screen or the game does not start**: update your graphics card drivers.
- **New versions**: at start-up the game checks GitHub for a newer version and, if there is one, shows it at the bottom right of the main menu with the Download, What's new, Later and Skip this version buttons. This needs an internet connection; without one, the game starts as usual. The check can be turned off in Settings → New versions.
- **Windows SmartScreen warning**: the installer is not signed. Click "More info" → "Run anyway".

The project **offers no support**: this guide and the source code are all the documentation available.
